# Literature review, wave 4: methods for a noisy within-account ranking problem (2022-2026)

Date: 2026-11-19. Extends `research/06_literature.md` (2026-11-07); papers already covered there are referenced only where I add or disagree.

**Link status.** `[V]` = arXiv id confirmed through the arXiv API (title and first author matched). `[S]` = id or URL seen in search results, page not opened. `[U]` = link unverified. `[mem]` = number quoted from memory, re-check before reuse. I opened no full paper in this pass. Claimed gains come from abstracts and search summaries; for the well-known methods papers of sections 5 to 12 the one-line description of the claim is from my memory of the paper, with only the id checked. Length: about 6,500 words counting links and table markup, above the 4,500 target.

**How to read "author-controlled".** "No" means a random or temporal post-level split in which the same users appear in train and test, and the score (Spearman, MAE on absolute log-popularity) is dominated by between-user differences. Our metric removes that entirely: both posts of a pair share the account, topic and week. A gain reported under "No" says almost nothing about our benchmark.

**What a method has to do here.** Thirteen tracks showed that adding inputs or capacity gives 0 on pair accuracy (about 60% overall, ceiling about 80%). The one lever that moved was the ordering of pairs by how likely the call is right (confident share on 2x pairs 26% to 37% at 73.6%). So each "transfer" verdict below separates (a) pair accuracy from (b) confident coverage, which is a selective-prediction quantity (area under the risk-coverage curve), not an accuracy quantity.

## Where I disagree with or add to `06_literature.md`

1. **Upworthy "82-84%".** The existing review cites a fine-tuned Llama-3-8B at 82-84% on significant pairs (MSI report). The published LOLA abstract (Marketing Science 2025) says embedding models and the fine-tuned Llama-3-8B are "marginally" above random, and the arXiv v1 says prompting stays under 65% (both from search summaries). The two can only both hold if 82-84% is on a strongly filtered subset. Treat 82-84% as the analogue of our 5x-gap band (we already get 80.8% on confident 5x pairs), not as evidence that a fine-tuned LLM would lift the 2x band.
2. **Retrieval (H2 item 6, "+27-48%").** Those gains are on random-split SMPD/ICIP-type data, where retrieved neighbours carry user and topic popularity. We already pool 16 neighbours; in a same-account same-topic pair the retrieved sets are nearly identical for both posts and cancel. Expect 0 on the benchmark.
3. **Heavy-tail objectives (H2 item 9).** Huber/Tweedie are superseded by the HL-Gauss heads we have. The open question is no longer the likelihood but whether the predicted *spread* is used when stating pair confidence. It is not: confidence is one global slope on the difference of means.
4. **Pair training (H2 item 2).** The literature result (Ding 2019, PopALM) is on accuracy; ours replicated only on coverage. The literature in sections 5 and 11 explains why: a pair loss on noisy labels mainly reshapes the score scale.

## 5. Learning to rank with noisy pairwise labels

Our loss: RankNet on the difference of predicted means, weighted by gap, near-ties dropped.

| Paper | Claim, data | Transfer here |
|---|---|---|
| Rao and Kupper, JASA 1967; Davidson, JASA 1970 (classical, no link). Modern use: Chen et al., *On extending DPO to accommodate ties*, NeurIPS 2025, [arXiv 2409.17431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17431) [S] | Bradley-Terry with an explicit tie probability; Rao-Kupper win probability is a sigmoid of (margin minus a tie parameter). Adding tied pairs under this model does not hurt and regularises; adding them to plain Bradley-Terry does hurt. | Direct. We *drop* near-ties (about 19% of benchmark pairs are under 1.25x). Keeping them with a three-outcome likelihood (A wins, tie, B wins; tie = within 1.25x) teaches the model that a small predicted gap is the right answer for many pairs, which is what separates confident from unsure calls. Accuracy 0; coverage plausibly +1 to +3. |
| *Noise tolerance of learning to rank under class-conditional label noise*, 2022, [arXiv 2208.02126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02126) [S]; PeerRank, IEEE Access 2022, [link](https://doaj.org/article/ff7a5b2f94a04fa8957965b37525b601) [S]; earlier SIGIR 2018 result that pairwise hinge and L1 are symmetric and hence noise-tolerant | Under symmetric flip noise the *minimiser* of a symmetric pairwise loss is unchanged; logistic loss is not symmetric, so its minimiser is pulled by noise. Gains shown on synthetic flips of LETOR data. | Our noise is a flip whose rate depends on the true gap (near 50% for small true gaps, low for large). Under that noise logistic loss is still *calibration-consistent*: it learns P(A beats B), which is what we want to state. A symmetric loss would keep the ordering but destroy the probability. Do not switch; at most use a symmetric loss for a ranking-only member. Expect 0. |
| Cheng et al., *RIME: robust preference-based RL with noisy preferences*, ICML 2024, [arXiv 2402.17257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17257) [V]; Lee et al., *B-Pref*, 2021, [arXiv 2111.03026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03026) [V] | Reward learning from pairwise labels with 10-30% flipped: a KL-threshold filter drops or flips pairs the model strongly disagrees with; large gains over plain Bradley-Terry at 30% noise on control tasks. | The closest setting in the literature (noisy pairs, small model). But RIME's noise is *mislabelling* of a true preference; ours is an honest draw of a random outcome. Filtering honest draws biases probabilities upward (overconfidence). Could raise coverage on paper while breaking calibration; any trial must report accuracy at fixed coverage. Expect 0 to +1 at 40% coverage, with risk. |
| Listwise: ListMLE (Xia et al., ICML 2008), ListNet (Cao et al., ICML 2007), ApproxNDCG, Pobrotyn and Białobrzeski, *NeuralNDCG*, 2021, [arXiv 2102.07831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07831) [V]; Oosterhuis, *PL-Rank*, SIGIR 2021, [arXiv 2105.00855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00855) [V]; Bai et al., *Regression-compatible listwise objectives for calibrated ranking*, KDD 2023, [arXiv 2211.01494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01494) [V] | Listwise losses beat pairwise by small margins on web search (about 0.5 NDCG points). Bai et al.: a softmax ranking loss conflicts with a regression loss on the same logits unless made compatible; the compatible form improves both. | Our episodes have several candidates per account, so a Plackett-Luce loss is available. Our measure is pairwise, the lists are short and the labels noisy: expect at most +0.3. The useful point is Bai's: the pair loss sits on *means of the HL-Gauss heads*, and the two losses want different scales. That is the documented effect of the fine-tune (slope 3.30 to 1.81). |
| Soft pair labels from gap size (no single canonical paper; label smoothing analysis in Müller et al., NeurIPS 2019, [arXiv 1906.02629](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02629) [V]) | Replace the 0/1 target with the probability that the observed winner is the true winner. | We can estimate that probability rather than guess it. From the repost study, the log-ratio of two draws of the same post has a known spread s (within 2x 62% of the time). For an observed log-ratio r, target = Phi(r / s) under a normal approximation, or the empirical curve. This replaces both the gap weights and the near-tie cut with one principled target. Accuracy at most +0.5; coverage +1 to +3. Same family as Rao-Kupper; do one, not both. |

## Synthesis

1. **Nothing in the 2022-2026 literature promises more pair accuracy on author-controlled data.** Every large reported gain is on splits where user identity leaks. On randomised or author-controlled pairs (Upworthy, Tan-Lee-Pang descendants) fine-tuned models stay in the low 60s on all pairs. Our 60 to 61% is in line with the field.
2. **The goal is a selective-prediction problem with aleatoric noise.** BALToR gives the optimal rule (threshold the pair's conditional risk). Feng et al. and our replicate track agree that selection heads and ensemble disagreement do not help. What remains is estimating each pair's noise: P(A beats B) = Phi(difference / pair noise), where pair noise varies by account, by size and by count level, and we currently use one slope.
3. **Noise handling should preserve probabilities.** Tie-aware or soft-target likelihoods fit; bounded losses, sample filtering and feature-ordering regularisers do not.
4. **Check the benchmark's gap definition at low counts** before more modelling. If many "2x" pairs are 1 like against 3, part of the gap to 75% is Poisson noise that no model can remove, and the ceiling for the 2x band is lower than 85%.

**Not recommended** (expected 0 or negative, reasons above): generalised or symmetric cross-entropy, co-teaching and small-loss filtering, RankSim/ConR/ordinal entropy, LDS/Balanced MSE for the pair task, evidential regression, a SelectiveNet-style head, more ensemble members or disagreement features, MAML-style per-account steps, MMoE/PLE, cascade models, further cross-account retrieval.

**Honest summary of the table.** Items 1 to 4 cost under a day together, need no training, and address the measured goal directly; I expect them to move coverage at 73 to 75% accuracy from 37% to roughly 40 to 45% if account-level noise differs between accounts, and to do nothing if it does not. Item 1's diagnostic settles that in an hour. Nothing on the list is expected to raise accuracy on all pairs by more than about a point.
