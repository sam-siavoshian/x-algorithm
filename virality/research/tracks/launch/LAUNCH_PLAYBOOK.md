# Launch playbook: what the data supports for startup and company posts on X

Built 2026-11-16 from the product's own corpus. Every number below is measured on that corpus; nothing here is
general advice. Where the data cannot answer a question, this page says so and gives no recommendation.

## How to read this

**What was measured.** 5,179 launch posts and 3,130 other own announcements (feature releases, funding, hiring,
open-source releases, milestones) found in 4.48 million posts. 270 accounts were identified as companies and 528 as
founders or individual builders. Most of the evidence comes from accounts with more than 10,000 followers; accounts
under 1,000 followers are almost absent (18 launch posts).

**The yardstick.** Each post is compared with the same account's own usual result: its likes divided by the median
likes of that account's previous 30 posts. "1.5x" means one and a half times what that account normally gets. A
difference between two ways of writing a launch post is always measured inside the same account, never between a big
account and a small one.

**Likes, not views.** View counts exist for only 205 of the company and founder launch posts, too few to measure any
of the effects below on views. Everything in section 2 is an effect on likes.

**Three numbers per finding.**
- *Win rate*: of two launch posts by the same account, one with the trait and one without, how often the one with it
  did better against the account's usual. Posting order is balanced. 50% means no effect.
- *Likes ratio*: likes with the trait divided by likes without, inside accounts that have both.
- *Adjusted ratio*: the same, holding every other trait fixed (so video is not credited with what layout did).

Brackets are 95% ranges from resampling accounts. A finding is stated only if it passes a rule fixed in advance: all
three numbers must exclude "no effect" in the main set of posts or in a stricter set that removes mislabelled posts,
and hold in the same direction in the other. Each finding was then re-measured separately before and after
23 April 2026.

**Grades.** A: passes the rule and is confirmed separately in both periods. A-: passes in the main set and in both
periods, weaker in the strict set. B: passes the rule; in one of the two periods the sample is too small to confirm it
on its own. C: holds only when launch posts of every account in the corpus are pooled.

These are associations inside accounts, not experiments. Roughly 30 traits were tested, so one weak finding passing
by chance would not be surprising; the weakest one that passed is marked.

## 1. What to expect from each kind of post

Likes relative to the account's own usual, company and founder accounts together.

| Kind of post | Typical result | Beats the account's usual | Lands in the account's own top 10% | Posts / accounts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Funding announcement | 6.9x (5.0 to 9.5) | 96% (91 to 100) | 50% | 74 / 61 |
| Open-source release | 2.6x (1.7 to 4.1) | 68% (55 to 79) | 29% | 62 / 54 |
| Product launch | 2.2x (2.1 to 2.4) | 71% (68 to 74) | 29% | 2,156 / 563 |
| Milestone or metric | 2.0x (1.2 to 3.2) | 73% (56 to 89) | 17% | 30 / 25, thin |
| Hiring | 1.3x (1.0 to 1.7) | 55% (46 to 63) | 15% | 176 / 120 |
| Feature release | 1.2x (1.1 to 1.3) | 55% (51 to 59) | 14% | 691 / 302 |

- A launch post is the account's strongest regular format: about seven in ten beat the account's usual, and nearly
  three in ten land in its own top tenth. On views (202 posts, 60 accounts) the lift is smaller: 1.5x (1.3 to 2.0),
  with 57% beating the usual.
- Funding announcements are the largest single event an account posts.
- Feature releases barely move. On company accounts they do no better than an ordinary post: 0.95x (0.80 to 1.11),
  208 posts. On founder accounts they do slightly better: 1.3x (1.1 to 1.5), 483 posts.
- Hiring posts are not distinguishable from an ordinary post on company accounts (0.8x, 0.5 to 1.3, 50 posts).
- Company account or founder account: launch posts do equally well relative to each account's own usual, 2.2x
  (2.0 to 2.4) on company accounts and 2.3x (2.1 to 2.6) on founder accounts.

## 2. What separates the stronger launch posts

Main set: 1,980 launch posts of 530 company and founder accounts.

| Grade | In the stronger version | Win rate | Likes ratio | Adjusted | Pairs / accounts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | The first line names the product | 64.0% (59.0 to 69.0) | 1.51x (1.27 to 1.78) | 1.36x (1.16 to 1.60) | 1,672 / 180 |
| A- | The text is laid out on three or more lines | 59.2% (54.4 to 63.8) | 1.37x (1.18 to 1.59) | 1.34x (1.18 to 1.56) | 1,631 / 144 |
| B | A video or GIF instead of an image | 62.8% (56.0 to 69.3) | 1.61x (1.32 to 1.96) | not defined | 1,021 / 113 |
| B | A video or GIF instead of no media | 65.4% (60.2 to 70.4) | 1.64x (1.33 to 2.02) | 1.58x (1.35 to 1.90) | 991 / 133 |
| B | The first line is the announcement, not a problem statement or a question | 72.5% (59.5 to 81.8) | 1.91x (1.33 to 2.65) | 1.73x (1.22 to 2.38) | 379 / 48 |
| B | An original post, not a quote post | 72.1% (60.7 to 81.1) | 1.90x (1.33 to 2.62) | 1.60x (1.11 to 2.23) | 363 / 65 |
| B | No external link in the post | 58.2% (50.4 to 65.7) | 1.29x (1.05 to 1.65) | 1.21x (0.99 to 1.53) | 927 / 95 |
| B, weakest | The first line contains a number | 55.8% (50.5 to 60.9) | 1.28x (1.06 to 1.52) | 1.18x (1.00 to 1.39) | 1,684 / 151 |
| C | At least 2 hours after the account's previous post | 57.9% (50.5 to 65.0) | 1.41x (1.14 to 1.72) | 1.33x (1.09 to 1.58) | 1,089 / 122, all accounts |

What each line means in practice, and how far it holds:

1. **Name the product in the first line (A).** "Introducing Atlas: ..." rather than a lead-in that gets to the name
   later. The only finding confirmed on its own in both periods: 58.8% (52.3 to 64.7) before 23 April 2026 and 72.8%
   (64.1 to 80.3) after. It holds for accounts under 100,000 followers (likes ratio 1.41x, 1.15 to 1.74) and above
   (1.49x, 1.17 to 1.89).

2. **Give the announcement room: three or more lines (A-).** Confirmed in both periods (60.9% and 61.1%). Among posts
   that already open with "Introducing <name>" the effect is smaller and not provable on its own (53.1%, 45.9 to
   59.9), so treat it as a modest gain, not a large one. Total length shows no effect (see section 3): it is layout,
   not word count.

3. **If there is media, make it a video or GIF (B).** Video beats an image in the main set and in the strict set
   (67.1%, 58.2 to 76.0). Video against no media is strongest for accounts under 100,000 followers: 74.9% (67.3 to
   81.4), likes ratio 2.58x (1.81 to 3.69), 252 pairs from 59 accounts. Two cautions. It was large before April 2026
   (73.6%, 66.5 to 79.0) and since then is in the same direction but not provable on its own (59.4%, 41.0 to 78.2,
   231 pairs). And a still image is **not** established as better than no media for a launch post: 59.5% in pairs,
   but the likes ratio is 1.17x (0.95 to 1.45), and among clear launch posts it is 48.0% (36.4 to 59.6).

4. **Open with the announcement, not with the problem (B).** Launch posts whose first line states a problem or asks
   a question did about half as well as the same account's other launch posts. Confirmed before April 2026 (69.3%,
   56.8 to 79.7); since then only 52 pairs exist. Few accounts do this (69 such posts against 419), so the range is
   wide.

5. **Make the launch an original post (B).** A launch written as a quote of another post did about half as well.
   Ordinary quote posts by these accounts get 0.84x their usual, so the launch-specific penalty is larger than the
   general one. Confirmed before April 2026 (68.1%, 58.7 to 76.8); 78 pairs since.

6. **Leave the link out of the launch post (B).** Posts without an external link won 58.2% of pairs against the same
   account's launch posts with one. The adjusted ratio (1.21x, 0.99 to 1.53) just includes no effect in the main set;
   in the strict set all three numbers pass (63.3%, 56.2 to 70.3; adjusted 1.36x, 1.05 to 1.86). Whether putting the
   link in a reply recovers the clicks could not be measured (section 4).

7. **A number in the first line (B, weakest).** A small effect, 55.8%, with a lower bound barely above 50%. Of the
   findings that passed, this is the one most likely to be chance.

8. **Do not launch within 2 hours of your previous post (C).** Launch posts that followed another post by under two
   hours got 0.71x (0.58 to 0.87) the likes of the same account's other launch posts; 267 such posts, 135 accounts.
   In the main set alone the pair test is not conclusive (55.6%, 44.8 to 65.0). This matches the product's existing
   finding for all posts (0.72x on views) and the same-author decay in X's ranking code.

## 3. Tested, no effect found

For these the data shows no difference that survives. They are listed so that nobody spends effort on them on the
strength of this corpus.

| Trait | Win rate | Likes ratio | Pairs / accounts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marked as a thread (🧵, 1/, "thread") | 51.2% (45.5 to 56.9) | 1.06x (0.88 to 1.27) | 1,635 / 146 |
| First line states a result (benchmark, "x times faster", #1) | 49.4% (38.2 to 60.8) | 1.08x (0.79 to 1.55) | 623 / 49 |
| 280 characters or longer | 57.1% (51.2 to 63.4) | 1.10x (0.92 to 1.30); adjusted 0.95x | 1,462 / 151 |
| 600 characters or longer, against under 280 | 51.7% (39.5 to 63.1) | 0.99x (0.73 to 1.28) | 453 / 73 |
| An image instead of no media | 59.5% (54.0 to 64.9) | 1.17x (0.95 to 1.45); adjusted 1.09x | 990 / 125 |
| Has an emoji | 53.2% (47.7 to 58.2) | 1.04x (0.88 to 1.24) | 1,197 / 132 |
| Tags three or more accounts, against none | 50.9% (40.2 to 63.4) | 1.20x (0.82 to 1.78) | 311 / 39 |
| Weekend against weekday (UTC) | 49.0% (34.8 to 62.2) | 0.87x (0.58 to 1.27) | 490 / 67 |
| Tuesday to Thursday against Monday and Friday | 50.9% (45.1 to 56.9) | 0.97x (0.85 to 1.12) | 1,770 / 176 |
| 13:00 to 17:59 UTC against any other hour | 54.5% (49.0 to 60.0) | 1.18x (1.01 to 1.38) | 1,893 / 181 |

Three leanings that did not pass and carry no recommendation: tagging any other account (the version without tags
won 59.1%, 52.9 to 64.4, but the likes ratio 1.18x, 0.99 to 1.41, includes no effect); opening with the announcement
verb itself ("Introducing", "Announcing": 59.9%, but nothing once naming the product is held fixed, adjusted 1.09x,
0.92 to 1.28); and 18:00 to 23:59 UTC doing worse than 13:00 to 17:59 UTC (56.8% in the main set, 48.6% in the
strict set, so it does not hold).

One trait looks strong and is not a lever: launch posts followed by another post from the same account within two
hours did 1.67x better (69.1%). A launch that is taking off gets followed up; the follow-up is the result, not the
cause.

## 4. What could not be measured

| Question | Why not |
|---|---|
| Link in a reply against link in the post | Replies are not in the corpus. Only 43 launch posts say the link is in the reply or below; 17 pairs. |
| Thread against single post | The body of a thread is not in the corpus; only the head post's marker was tested (no effect, section 3). |
| Any effect on views | 205 company and founder launch posts have view counts; no trait has enough accounts with both versions. |
| Hashtags | 41 launch posts with a hashtag; 240 pairs from 30 accounts. The version without won 60.2% (43.4 to 76.6): too thin to state. |
| Same launch, same topic, two wordings | Only 44 such pairs with a launch post on one side. |
| The same launch from the founder's account and the company's account | No way to tell who belongs to which company (profile bios are empty in the corpus). See below for the one thing that could be measured. |
| Whether a second account quote-posting the launch lifts the launch itself | Launches that are doing well attract quotes, so the comparison is circular. |
| Best local day and hour | The audience's time zone is unknown; day and hour were tested in UTC only. |
| Accounts under 1,000 followers | 18 launch posts. |

What can be said about quote-posting a launch from a second account: a first-person quote post of someone else's
launch ("we just shipped ...") did 1.70x (1.47 to 2.01) the quoting account's own usual, and 1.44x (1.27 to 1.67)
that account's other quote posts; 560 posts, 401 accounts. So it is a good post for the person who makes it. It is a
small post next to the original: it got more likes than the original in 5% of 493 cases. Where the quoting account
could be tied to the company (it quotes that company's announcements in the first person at least twice: 152 cases,
30 people, 19 companies), the picture is the same: 2.4x (1.5 to 3.8) the person's usual, and more likes than the
company's own post in 10% of cases.

## 5. How far to trust the product's predictor on launch posts

- The existing benchmark says almost nothing about launches: of its 1,660 held-out pairs, 3 have a launch post on
  either side.
- A new held-out test was built: 850 pairs of launch posts by the same account, all posted after 23 April 2026,
  from 151 accounts. The current model picked the better one 63.7% of the time (59.5 to 68.0). When the two differ
  by at least 2x in likes: 66.0% (60.9 to 71.6) of 568 pairs.
- When the model states at least 60% confidence (26% of the pairs) it is right 73.3% of the time (65.3 to 81.0), and
  78.3% (69.4 to 87.2) on pairs that differ by 2x or more.
- The model does not know that a launch is a launch. It expects these posts to get 1.03x the account's usual; they
  got 2.19x (576 posts). Its forecast of the number of likes for a launch post is therefore about half of what
  happens. Its ranking of one launch draft against another is usable; its absolute forecast for a launch is not.
- In these newest pairs the version that named the product in the first line won 73.9% of 264 pairs; the model
  preferred it 61.4% of the time. The version with a video won 50.5% of 220 pairs; the model preferred it 67.7% of
  the time.

## Limits

- Likes only, for every effect in section 2.
- Observational. Accounts choose when to use video or how to open; a finding here is "inside the same account, posts
  written this way did better", not proof that rewriting a draft will produce the gain.
- Company and founder accounts were identified from display names and posting patterns, because profile bios are
  missing. Read by hand, about three in four "company" accounts are product companies or labs, and about six in ten
  "founder" accounts are people announcing their own products. Post labels are rules checked by reading samples:
  launch 19 of 25 correct, funding 17 of 20, hiring 15 of 20, feature release 16 of 25, milestone 17 of 25.
- The accounts are mostly technology and AI companies with large followings.

## Outside context (not evidence for anything above)

No peer-reviewed study isolating startup launch posts on X was found. A within-account analysis of Threads reported
video about 20% above images. A vendor study of X "build in public" posts (22 launch posts) reported that top posts
more often opened with a number. X's own marketing material claims 10x engagement for video, without controls. The
product's general evidence (research/11_reasons_and_fixes.md) cites Tan, Lee and Pang (2014) for the same-author
design used here.
